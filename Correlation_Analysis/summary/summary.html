TESS cluster ages · correlation analysis · 348 clusters (MW 124, LMC 118, SMC 106)

The light curves carry no age information beyond the nuisance parameters

After removing galaxy and observing setup, the strongest light-curve statistic tracks age at ρ = −0.26 [−0.37, −0.15], about 7% of the rank variance. Adding all 23 light-curve features to a random forest does not improve age prediction beyond a nuisance-only model, either for log age (ΔR² = +0.06, p = 0.16) or for young/old at 10⁸ yr (ΔAUC = −0.004, p = 0.53).

How to state it. There are weak, monotonic trends: younger clusters are somewhat more variable. These trends are statistically detectable but small next to the cluster-to-cluster scatter. They give no predictive power that the galaxy, cadence, sector count, noise floor, brightness, aperture and sky position do not already provide.

1 · Linear: trends exist, but they are small next to the scatter

  • Controlling for nuisances shrinks the strongest raw correlation, variability amplitude, from −0.37 to −0.26. The galaxy offset accounts for about a third of it: LMC clusters are young, and the LMC sample tops out at log age 8.8.
  • Two families appear only after control: noise redness (−0.21) and GP jitter (−0.13). Both fail the within-galaxy shuffle test (FDR p = 1.0 and 0.52), so they depend on the linear nuisance model and are not robust.
  • Within each galaxy, amplitude gives ρ = −0.25 (LMC), −0.30 (MW) and −0.22 (SMC). The sign is consistent, but each value comes from only about 100–120 clusters.

2 · Random forest: no predictive gain beyond the nuisances

  • The full-minus-nuisance R² gain varies by ±0.12 from fold to fold, more than twice its mean of +0.046. On the held-out clusters the sign reverses: the nuisance-only model scores higher.
  • For young/old classification, the nuisance-only forest already reaches AUC 0.74 because galaxy predicts the label: 73% of MW, 70% of SMC and 47% of LMC clusters are old. The light curves add nothing on top. Their held-out +0.12 is a single 70-cluster draw, and cross-validation does not reproduce it.
  • The same null holds for every variant tried: training on individual sector combinations instead of cluster medians (ΔR² +0.13 vs null +0.06 ± 0.09, p = 0.36), one representative feature per family, and the 10-bin GP power spectrum in place of summary statistics. None beats the nuisance-only model on the held-out clusters.

Caveats

  • Sample size limits the bound. With 348 clusters, a light-curve gain needs to exceed about 0.075 in R² or in AUC to clear the 95th percentile of the shuffled distributions. Anything smaller cannot be told apart from the null. This is a non-detection at that level, not proof that the gain is zero.
  • Per-galaxy forests are mixed. Trained within one galaxy, the light curves add ΔR² ≈ +0.18 in the LMC and in the SMC (about 90 clusters each) but −0.01 in the MW, which has by far the widest age range. These fits had no permutation null and use small samples.
  • The mixed-model cross-check is not usable. The row-level mixed model in 01_linear_correlations returned degenerate fits (coefficient 0, standard error 0). Do not cite it.
